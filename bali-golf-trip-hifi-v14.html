Which Bali course should you play first?

12 August 2026 · 6 minute read

If you have one morning, play New Kuta. If you have three days and a group with mixed handicaps, the order changes — and so does the answer about transport.

New Kuta is the course people picture when they picture golf in Bali: limestone cliff, links turf, and holes that finish with nothing behind the green but water. Ronald Fream built it in 2007 and the Asian Tour brought the Indonesia Open here two years later. It also has the tightest tee windows of the three, which makes it the one to book first and plan the rest of the trip around.

Bukit Pandawa is eighteen par 3s and takes half the time — the World Golf Awards called it the best par 3 course in the world in 2017. That makes it the right call for a group where not everyone plays, or for the afternoon of a day that started somewhere else. Handara is the outlier: two hours north, laid inside a volcanic crater, cool enough for a jumper at dawn, and worth the drive precisely because it feels nothing like the rest of the island.

The 15th at New Kuta plays across a gully with the Indian Ocean behind it. Nobody remembers their score on that hole.

Whichever order you choose, book the early window. By nine in the morning in August the heat has arrived and the light has gone flat.
